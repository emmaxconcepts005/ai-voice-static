Retell AI vs Vapi: which is better for voice agents?

An honest comparison from someone who builds voice agents on both platforms, and how to pick the right one for your business.

Retell AI and Vapi are two of the most popular platforms for building AI voice agents, and I build on both. They can produce excellent agents, but they suit different situations. Here's how I think about choosing between them.

Note: Both platforms update their features and pricing frequently. Treat this as a guide to how they differ, and check each platform's current documentation before deciding.

The short answer

  • Choose Retell AI when you want a reliable, natural-sounding phone agent set up quickly, with a strong visual builder and less engineering.
  • Choose Vapi when you need maximum flexibility: choosing your own AI models and voice providers, complex tool calls and custom behaviour controlled through code.

For most small and medium service businesses (receptionists, booking and lead qualification), either works well. The bigger difference is usually how the agent is designed and tested, not the platform.

Retell AI: strengths

  • Fast to a good result. Natural conversations out of the box, with sensible defaults for interruptions and turn-taking.
  • Visual conversation flows. Useful for structured calls like booking and qualification, where you want predictable steps.
  • Phone-call focused. Built around phone agents, with telephony and call handling as a core feature.
  • Easier to hand over. Non-technical teams can usually follow and adjust what the agent does.

Vapi: strengths

  • Highly configurable. You can choose and combine different speech-to-text, language model and voice providers.
  • Developer-friendly. A strong API and tool (function) calling make it well suited to custom integrations.
  • Flexible architecture. Good for advanced setups, such as several specialised assistants working together.
  • Web and phone. Works for phone calls and voice in websites or apps.

What matters more than the platform

In practice, whether an agent works well depends far more on:

  • The conversation design: clear goals, short questions and good handling when callers go off-script.
  • Integrations: the agent is only useful if bookings and details reach your calendar and CRM reliably.
  • Testing: running real scenarios, including difficult calls, before launch.
  • Monitoring: reviewing real calls and improving the agent after launch.

How I choose for a client

  1. What does the call need to do? Simple, structured calls lean towards Retell AI. Complex, branching logic with lots of tool calls leans towards Vapi.
  2. Who will maintain it? If your team wants to adjust it themselves, a more visual setup helps.
  3. Which tools does it connect to? Both connect to most CRMs through webhooks and automation tools like n8n or Make.com.
  4. Running cost at your call volume. Compare current per-minute pricing with the models and voices you'd actually use.

You can watch recorded Retell AI voice agent demos on my projects page, and I've delivered client projects on both platforms.

Frequently asked questions

Is Retell AI or Vapi cheaper?

It depends on the AI models, voices and telephony you choose and your call volume. Pricing changes often, so compare current per-minute rates for your exact setup.

Can both connect to my CRM?

Yes. Both can send and receive data through webhooks and function or tool calls, often through n8n, Make.com or Zapier, to CRMs such as GoHighLevel, HubSpot and Pipedrive.

Can I switch platforms later?

Yes, though the agent has to be rebuilt and retested on the new platform. Keeping your integrations in a separate automation tool makes switching easier.

Which do you recommend?

It depends on what your calls need to do. I'll recommend one during solution design and explain why.
