How much does an AI receptionist cost?

A clear breakdown of setup costs, monthly running costs and the factors that change both, written by someone who builds AI receptionists.

"How much does it cost?" is the first question almost everyone asks about AI receptionists, and the honest answer is: it depends on a few specific things. This guide breaks down exactly what those things are, so you can estimate your own cost and compare quotes properly.

The two parts of the cost

Every AI receptionist has two kinds of cost:

  1. Setup (one-off). Designing the call flow, writing the agent's instructions, connecting it to your calendar and CRM, testing and launching. This is usually the bigger number up front.
  2. Running costs (monthly). What you pay the platforms to actually handle calls: usage per minute, phone numbers and any software subscriptions involved.

When you compare options, always ask about both. A cheap setup with expensive running costs, or the other way round, can end up costing very different amounts over a year.

What affects the setup cost

Setup cost depends mostly on how much the agent needs to do, not on how long the calls are. The main factors:

  • Number of call types. An agent that only books appointments is simpler than one that also handles quotes, complaints, emergencies and FAQs.
  • Integrations. Each system the agent connects to (calendar, CRM, job management software, SMS or WhatsApp) adds design and testing work.
  • Booking logic. Checking availability across several staff, service areas or job types takes more work than a single calendar.
  • Inbound, outbound or both. Outbound calling (for example, calling new leads or reminders) is a separate flow to design.
  • Follow-up automation. Confirmations, reminders, CRM updates and team alerts after the call.
  • Testing depth. More scenarios mean more testing, but this is where reliability comes from. Don't skip it.
Tip: Start with the one or two call types that cost you the most when they're missed. You can add more later once the agent is proving itself.

What affects the monthly running cost

Running costs come from the platforms the agent runs on. Typically:

  • Voice platform usage. Platforms like Retell AI and Vapi charge per minute of conversation. The per-minute rate depends on the platform and the AI models and voices chosen.
  • Phone number and call minutes. A phone number from a telephony provider such as Twilio, plus their per-minute calling charges.
  • Automation tools. If the agent connects through n8n, Make.com or Zapier, those have their own plans based on usage.
  • Your CRM. Usually something you already pay for.

Platform prices change often, so always check the current pricing pages rather than trusting an old article (including this one) for exact numbers.

How to estimate your own monthly cost

You can get a rough estimate with a simple calculation:

Calls per month × average call length (minutes) × total cost per minute
+ phone number + automation subscriptions

For example, if you get 300 calls a month averaging 3 minutes, that's 900 minutes. Multiply by the combined per-minute rate of your voice platform and telephony provider, then add the fixed monthly costs. Short, focused calls keep this number down, which is one reason a well-designed agent matters.

How to compare it with a human receptionist

An AI receptionist isn't always a replacement for a person. Often it covers the gaps: after hours, weekends, busy periods and overflow when your team is already on the phone. When you compare costs, think about:

  • How many calls you currently miss, and what an average job or booking is worth to you
  • Hours your team spends answering the same questions
  • Whether you need cover outside office hours

If a single missed job is worth a lot to your business, even a small number of recovered calls each month can cover the running cost.

Costs people forget

  • Changes after launch. Your services, prices or rules will change. Ask how updates are handled.
  • Monitoring. Someone should review calls, especially in the first weeks.
  • Platform lock-in. Ask who owns the accounts. Ideally the voice platform, phone number and automations are in your name.

Questions to ask before you buy

  1. What exactly is included in the setup, and what isn't?
  2. What will my monthly running cost be, based on my call volume?
  3. Whose accounts will the system run on?
  4. How is it tested before launch?
  5. What happens when the agent can't handle a call?
  6. How are changes handled after launch?

Frequently asked questions

Is there a monthly fee for an AI receptionist?

Usually, yes. Beyond the one-off setup, you pay the voice platform and telephony provider for usage (typically per minute), plus any automation tool subscriptions.

Is an AI receptionist cheaper than a human?

For covering after-hours calls, overflow and repetitive questions it is often cheaper, but it works best alongside your team rather than as a full replacement.

Can I keep costs low?

Yes: start with one or two high-value call types, keep calls focused, and make sure the accounts are in your name so you only pay for what you use.
